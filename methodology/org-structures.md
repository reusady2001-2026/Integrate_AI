# Organizational Structures & Governance

No single best structure — fit it to size, age, environment, and strategy
(contingency view).

## Mintzberg's five configurations
Each = a dominant part + a prime coordinating mechanism + a decentralization type.

1. **Simple structure** — strategic apex; *direct supervision*. Fit: small/young, crisis, startups. Risk: founder-dependence, doesn't scale.
2. **Machine bureaucracy** — technostructure; *standardization of work processes*. Fit: mature, stable, high-volume (manufacturing, utilities). Risk: rigidity, slow adaptation.
3. **Professional bureaucracy** — operating core; *standardization of skills*. Fit: hospitals, universities, law/audit firms. Risk: weak central control, slow to innovate.
4. **Divisionalized form** — middle line; *standardization of outputs*. Fit: large diversified / multi-company groups. Risk: duplication, HQ–division tension, short-termism.
5. **Adhocracy** — support staff/operating core; *mutual adjustment*. Fit: project/R&D/innovation. Risk: inefficiency, ambiguity.

## Classic structure types
- **Functional** — efficient, deep expertise; risk: silos, slow cross-unit response.
- **Divisional** (product/geographic/market) — accountability, market focus; risk: duplication.
- **Matrix** — dual reporting (e.g., product × function); fit: genuinely need two dimensions at once; risk: power struggles, two-boss stress, slow decisions.
- **Network / virtual** — outsourced flexible core; risk: control/IP loss.
- **Team-based** — agility; risk: coordination.
- **Holacracy** — self-managing circles, no managers. *Flag: emerging/contested, vendor-originated (HolacracyOne); no academic consensus.*

## Galbraith Star Model
Align five levers — **Strategy → Structure, Processes, Rewards, People**. If any
lever contradicts strategy, performance fails. Use it as the design checklist.

## Governance by org type
- **Corporate** — unitary or two-tier board (per G20/OECD Principles) with audit, nomination, remuneration committees overseeing management.
- **Nonprofit** — board carries BoardSource's ten core responsibilities (set mission; select/support/evaluate the chief executive; ensure planning; financial & legal oversight).
- **Public sector** — accountability to elected authority; transparency; separation of policy from administration (jurisdiction-specific).
- **SOE** — OECD SOE Guidelines (insulate commercial management from political interference).

## Rules
1. **Fit structure to the situation** — match the coordinating mechanism to size, age, environment stability, technical system.
2. **Align all five Star points** — Strategy drives Structure, Processes, Rewards, People.
3. **Span of control:** widen for standardized/skilled work; narrow for complex, interdependent, low-standardization work (span trades off against levels).
4. **Delegate decision rights to where the information lives;** centralize for scale/consistency.
5. **Matrix only when two dimensions are genuinely needed** and you can fund conflict-resolution processes — otherwise functional or divisional.
6. **Separate new growth engines from the core** (a divisional/adhocracy unit) so a bureaucratic core doesn't smother innovation.
7. **Diversified multi-company group → divisionalized form** with output standardization (clear per-unit KPIs) and a lean HQ for capital allocation & risk.
8. **Match governance bodies to org type** (see above); give every executive box a single clear reporting line and a matching role definition.
