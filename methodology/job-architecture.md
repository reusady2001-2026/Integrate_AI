# Job Architecture & Role Definitions

## Occupational taxonomy (the authoritative skeleton)
- **US BLS SOC 2018** — 4-level hierarchy via 6-digit code: **23 major groups → 98 minor → 459 broad → 867 detailed** occupations. Management = major group **11-0000**; **Top Executives 11-1000**; **Chief Executives 11-1011**; General & Operations Managers 11-1021.
- **O*NET** (built on SOC) — Content Model with six domains: Worker Characteristics (abilities, interests, work styles), Worker Requirements (skills, knowledge, education), Experience Requirements, Occupational Requirements (work activities + context), Workforce Characteristics, Occupation-Specific Information (tasks, tools/technology). Use it to separate transferable **skills/knowledge** from occupation-specific **tasks** — never invent duties.

## Job-description components (SHRM + ADA/FLSA practice)
Title · purpose/summary (one line) · **essential functions** (vs. marginal, with frequency and consequence-of-failure) · reporting relationships · minimum vs. preferred qualifications (education, experience, competencies, licenses) · physical/work-context demands · **FLSA status** (exempt/non-exempt — determined by *actual duties*, not title). A description written *before* advertising is legal evidence of essential functions (EEOC).

## C-suite & senior-function mandates
CEO (overall strategy, board accountability) · CFO (finance, capital, reporting, controls) · COO (day-to-day operations/execution) · CTO (product/engineering tech) · CIO (internal IT/systems) · CHRO (people, org, talent) · CMO (marketing, brand, demand) · CLO/General Counsel (legal, compliance, governance) · CRO-Risk (enterprise risk) · CRO-Revenue (unified sales+marketing+CS) · CSO (strategy, corp-dev, M&A) · CISO (security) · CPO (product *or* procurement, context-dependent).

## Rules
1. **Anchor to taxonomy** — map each role to its SOC detailed occupation; pull tasks/skills/knowledge from O*NET. Gaps → `[להשלמה / TO FILL]`.
2. **Separate essential vs. marginal functions**, each as action-verb + object + purpose, with frequency.
3. **Purpose in one line** before duties; group duties under 4–7 responsibility headers; one duty per bullet.
4. **Classify FLSA by actual duties**, not title.
5. **Specify minimum vs. preferred qualifications** and competencies separately; tie competencies to a defined framework.
6. **Place every role in job architecture:** assign a **job family**, a **level/grade**, and a **track** — individual-contributor and manager ladders run **parallel** (don't force ICs through people-management to advance).
7. **Define reporting lines** up and down + key cross-functional interfaces.
8. **Scale the C-suite by size/stage:**
   - Startup/small (<~50): CEO + a finance lead + ops/tech founder cover most.
   - Mid-size (~50–500): add CFO, COO/CHRO, CTO/CIO as complexity grows.
   - Large/public/regulated: full suite incl. GC/CLO, CISO, CRO-risk, CSO.
   - **Heuristic:** create a C-role only when the function is (i) strategic *and* (ii) needs a dedicated voice at the top table — else fold it under an existing exec.
9. **Match the role set to org type:** regulated/financial → mandatory GC, CRO-risk, compliance; tech/product → CTO + CISO; sales-driven → CRO-revenue; manufacturing/real estate → strong COO.
10. **Keep descriptions tweak-ready and re-validated** against actual duties.
