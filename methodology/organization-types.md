# Organization Types & Industry Classification

The first step in any engagement: classify the organization. Type gates
governance, disclosure duties, the objective function, and which roles exist.

## 1. Legal / ownership forms (feature → implication)

| Form | Defining feature | Implication for strategy / governance / roles |
|---|---|---|
| Publicly traded | Dispersed shareholders; securities regulator + disclosure | Strategy answerable to markets/analysts; independent board + audit committee; needs IR, compliance, internal-audit roles |
| Privately held | Concentrated owners; light disclosure | Owner-driven, faster strategy; informal governance; overlapping roles, fewer formal controls |
| Holding / group | Parent owns subsidiaries | Two layers — **portfolio** (parent: capital allocation, shared services) vs. **operating** (subsidiary); group-level vs. unit-level roles & KPIs |
| Partnership (GP/LP/LLP) | Partners share profit/liability | Strategy by partner consensus; governance per partnership agreement; "roles" = partner tracks |
| LLC | Members; limited liability; flexible rules | Governance per operating agreement (member- vs. manager-managed) |
| Cooperative | Member-owned, one-member-one-vote | Strategy serves member benefit, not profit-max; democratic governance |
| Family-owned | Ownership + family + management overlap | Succession & family-vs-business tension central; add family council/charter; separate family vs. professional-manager roles |
| State-owned enterprise (SOE) | Government owner; policy + commercial mandate | Dual objectives; insulate from political interference (OECD SOE Guidelines); roles bridge ministry oversight & commercial mgmt |
| Nonprofit / NGO | No owners; mission-driven; surplus reinvested | Strategy = mission + funding sustainability; fiduciary volunteer board; program vs. development/fundraising roles |
| Government agency | Statutory mandate; public funds | Strategy bounded by legislation/budget; accountable to legislature/citizens; rule-bound civil-service grades |
| Startup / scale-up | Investor-backed; high growth/uncertainty | Strategy = product-market fit → scaling; lean board w/ VC seats; fluid roles, frequent redesign per stage |

## 2. Industry classification standards

- **GICS** (MSCI + S&P Dow Jones): 4 tiers — **11 sectors → industry groups → industries → sub-industries**. Investor/market lens. Classify by *principal business activity*.
- **NAICS** (US Census; w/ Canada & Mexico): **20 sectors**, hierarchical 2→6 digits (2=sector … 6=national industry). Production-process based.
- **ISIC** (UN Statistics Division): global statistical standard; Rev.4 = 21 sections / 88 divisions / 238 groups / 419 classes (Rev.5 endorsed 2023). Basis for many national systems.
- **SIC**: legacy US 4-digit system, largely superseded by NAICS (still in older SEC filings).

Record the sector **code + standard + edition** used.

## 3. By-sector restructuring nuances

- **Manufacturing** → supply chain, capacity, OEE/quality, safety.
- **Services** → talent, utilization, service quality.
- **Tech / SaaS** → product & R&D, unit economics (ARR, churn, LTV:CAC).
- **Financial** → risk, capital adequacy, heavy regulatory/compliance roles.
- **Healthcare** → clinical governance, accreditation, patient safety.
- **Real estate** → NOI, FFO, occupancy, cap rate, asset/portfolio management.
- **Nonprofit** → program impact + grant compliance.
- **Public sector** → procurement, transparency, statutory accountability.

## Rules
1. **Identify the legal/ownership form first** — it gates governance, disclosure, and who must approve the strategy.
2. **For holdings, always work two layers** (portfolio vs. operating); never blur parent and subsidiary roles/KPIs.
3. **Classify with the right standard for the purpose:** GICS for investor/benchmark framing; NAICS/ISIC for operational/statistical and cross-country comparison.
4. **Map governance bodies to form** (independent board+audit committee for public; family council for family firms; ministry oversight for SOE; fiduciary board for nonprofit).
5. **Tie the objective function to mandate:** profit/market for commercial; mission + funding for nonprofit; statutory/budget compliance for public; dual mandate for SOE.
6. **Benchmark only against same-class, same-form peers.**
7. **Scale role density to maturity:** lean/fluid for startups; formal controls (compliance, IR, internal audit) for public/regulated.
8. **Apply the sector nuance** above to choose which segments, roles, and KPIs matter.
