# Strategy Frameworks

Canonical frameworks with their originators and when each applies. Combine 2–4
complementary lenses; never stack redundant ones. SWOT is a *summary*, not an
analysis — feed it from the others.

| Framework | Originator / source | What it does | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five Forces | Porter, HBR 1979 / *Competitive Strategy* 1980 | Industry attractiveness via 5 forces | Whether/how to compete; structural profitability |
| Generic Strategies | Porter 1980 | Cost leadership vs. differentiation vs. focus | Choosing a defensible position; avoid "stuck in the middle" |
| Value Chain | Porter, *Competitive Advantage* 1985 | Primary/support activities → where margin & advantage sit | Cost/differentiation diagnosis |
| SWOT | SRI/Stanford (Humphrey et al., 1960s; from "SOFT") | Internal S/W × external O/T | Quick synthesis — a summary tool, not rigorous analysis |
| PESTEL | Rooted in Aguilar's *ETPS* (1967) | Political/Economic/Social/Technological/Environmental/Legal macro scan | External scanning, market entry, foresight |
| Ansoff Matrix | Ansoff, HBR 1957 | Penetration, product/market development, diversification | Choosing growth direction & risk appetite |
| BCG Growth-Share | Henderson, BCG 1970 | Portfolio by market growth × relative share | Multi-business capital allocation (never single-business) |
| VRIO / RBV | Barney, *J. of Mgmt* 1991 | Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Organized resources | Internal capability audit; durable advantage |
| Blue Ocean | Kim & Mauborgne, INSEAD, 2005 | Create uncontested market space (value innovation) | Saturated markets; demand creation |
| Balanced Scorecard / Strategy Maps | Kaplan & Norton, HBR 1992 / 2004 | Translate strategy into linked objectives | Execution, measurement, alignment |
| McKinsey 7S | Peters, Waterman, Pascale, Athos (late 1970s) | Align Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared values, Skills, Style, Staff | Org effectiveness, post-merger, change readiness |
| Scenario Planning | Shell / Pierre Wack, HBR 1985 | Multiple plausible futures to stress-test | Deep uncertainty, long horizons |
| Three Horizons | Baghai, Coley & White (McKinsey), 1999 | Manage core (H1), emerging (H2), future options (H3) | Balancing today's earnings with future growth |
| Theory of Change / Logic Model | Weiss / Aspen; W.K. Kellogg Guide 2004 | Inputs→activities→outputs→outcomes→impact | Nonprofit/public-sector strategy & evaluation |

## Selection rules
1. **Competitive positioning** → Five Forces + Value Chain + Generic Strategies + VRIO. Forces sets the ceiling; VRIO/value chain explain *your* slice.
2. **Growth question** → Ansoff (direction) + Three Horizons (timing) + Blue Ocean (if space is saturated).
3. **Multi-business portfolio** → BCG + Three Horizons. Never run BCG on a single-business firm.
4. **Turnaround** → Value Chain + 7S (find broken alignment) + BSC to re-anchor execution; PESTEL only if an external shock drove the decline.
5. **Always pair internal + external** (e.g., VRIO with Five Forces/PESTEL).
6. **High uncertainty / long horizon** → Scenario Planning + PESTEL; avoid single-period point tools.
7. **Execution & measurement** → Balanced Scorecard + Strategy Maps *after* the strategy is chosen.
8. **Nonprofit / public** → Theory of Change / Logic Model as primary; replace BSC's financial top layer with "mission impact."
9. **SWOT is downstream:** synthesize it from the analyses above; never start there.
10. **Ground competitive claims in real, named peers and real data** — if not in source, mark `[להשלמה / TO FILL]`; never invent competitors or figures.

## Flags
- **SWOT** origin is contested (Harvard attribution refuted by Humphrey); cite as "1960s SRI/Stanford corporate-planning research."
- **PESTEL** has no single named originator (descends from Aguilar's ETPS); PEST/STEEP are variants.
- **McKinsey 7S** is co-authored; no single originator.

## Sources
Porter (1980/1985, HBR 1979); Ansoff (HBR 1957); Barney (1991); Kim & Mauborgne (2005); Kaplan & Norton (HBR 1992/1996, *Strategy Maps* 2004); Baghai/Coley/White (1999, verified via McKinsey official); SWOT origin verified via peer-reviewed ScienceDirect; W.K. Kellogg / Aspen (Theory of Change).
